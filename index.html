AFT-Wisconsin Stands Firmly Against the Universities of Wisconsin’s Proposed General Education Plan

While the UW System claims a “legislative mandate” for its General Education proposal, Act 15 only requires the Board of Regents to “establish policies for the appropriate transfer of credits between institutions within the [UW] system.” Yet in “response” to this simple transfer requirement – accomplished by a straightforward policy requiring General Education credits taken at any UW institution to be accepted as General Education credits at any other UW institution – the UW System has proposed a rigid, one-size-fits-all General Education program for ALL UW campuses.

Faculty and students had no input on this proposal, which was created in secret and is the product of an unwritten, unverifiable “political deal” and not based in law or policy.

By dictating to our campuses, this proposal ignores state law, which affirms that “Subject to board policy the chancellors of the institutions in consultation with their faculties shall be responsible for designing curricula and setting degree requirements,” and further mandates that the Board of Regents “ensure the diversity of quality undergraduate programs” and “promote the widest degree of institutional autonomy...”

But this proposal prevents our campuses from adopting any unique requirements outside of or above the 36 credits dictated by the UW System.

Seemingly aware its own proposal is outside of the law, the UW System has recently – and stunningly — claimed that campus “autonomy is not subject to change.”

This proposal homogenizes our campuses, will require the immediate elimination of untold numbers of courses and faculty across our campuses, and will necessarily lead to a reduction in academic programs from which our students can choose.

This proposal usurps students’ right to learn.

This proposal violates the academic freedom of faculty and campus autonomy.

This proposal codifies a rigid, two-tiered system of teaching and learning by effectively exempting UW Madison and treating all other UW students and campuses as second class citizens.

If adopted, this proposal marks the beginning of the end of the eleven comprehensive campuses of the UW System and will drive Wisconsin students out of state.

The UW Board of Regents must stand up for students’ right to learn, the academic freedom of faculty, and the autonomy of our campuses and vote against this proposal!
